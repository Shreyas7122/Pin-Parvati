## The Last Night — Leaving

The days before leaving were busy.

There wasn't really any time to sit down and think about what I was about to do. I just kept doing whatever was left.

Pack this. Check that. Remove something. Add something else.

I had been told to keep my backpack around **7.5 kg**, but somehow, by the time I was done packing, it was closer to **10 kg**.

My flight was at 3 AM.

I planned to sleep at 10 PM and wake up around midnight.

Then I remembered something.

My phone storage was full.

So much for sleeping.

I spent the night deleting photos and videos, clearing storage and trying to make enough space for the expedition that I was about to photograph.

By the time I left for the airport, I hadn't really processed what was happening.

I just knew I was going.

At the airport, I started reading _Paths of Glory_.

The book begins with the discovery of a dead climber on Everest.

I was reading about Everest while sitting in an airport, about to fly towards my own Himalayan expedition.

Somewhere between the pages, the reality started catching up.

There was fear.

Excitement.

Fantasizing about what the next few days might look like.

And then there was something stranger — trying to imagine a future that I couldn't actually picture.

I had prepared for Pin Parvati for almost a year.

And now I was finally going there.

---

I reached Chandigarh around 6 AM.

I was supposed to meet my shared cab at 9, so I had a few hours to kill.

I was about to book a cab into the city when I stopped myself.

_I'm a traveller. I can figure this out._

So I walked.

Eventually, I found a bus.

I got in and, when the conductor asked where I wanted to go, I told him:

**"Bhaiji, last stop pe chhod dena."**

He looked slightly confused and tried asking where exactly I wanted to go.

I explained that I had a shared cab at 9 AM, so I just wanted to roam around for a while and eventually reach the pickup point.

And just like that, my wandering around Chandigarh began.

I started talking to a guy from Himachal who happened to be travelling on the same bus. We had one of those completely casual conversations that you have with strangers while travelling.

We got along well enough that we exchanged Instagram handles.

He actually lives near my office, so maybe that conversation wasn't going to end with the bus ride.

Then something happened that I still remember.

A man suddenly called out to the conductor.

**"Bhaiji, bhaiji, bachao."**

The conductor immediately turned around.

_"Kya hua?"_

The man couldn't really answer. He just signalled that his head was spinning.

The bus stopped.

The conductor called for an ambulance, but they were told it would take some time.

So the bus deviated from its route by around **2 kilometres** and headed towards the hospital.

When we reached the hospital, the man tried to take a step.

He fainted.

The conductor immediately called a nurse, got a wheelchair and escorted him inside to the doctor.

I was genuinely struck by the humanity he showed.

There was no drama about it.

He just saw someone who needed help and helped.

Later, the conductor came back and started talking to me casually again.

I told him,

**"It's rare to see people who care like this. You're doing a great job."**

We kept talking.

At some point, he asked me if I had a girlfriend.

I laughed.

**"I've been single since I was born."**

He laughed too and then asked for my Instagram.

Eventually, I told him where I was going.

I was going to attempt **Pin Parvati**.

He smiled and cheered me on.

**"Go get to the top."**

It was a small conversation with a stranger.

But somehow, that's how my expedition began.

Not with a mountain.

Not with a glacier.

Not even with the trek itself.

It began with a sleepless night, a book about Everest, a bus ride, a few strangers, and a conductor who took his bus 2 kilometres out of its way to help someone who needed it.
