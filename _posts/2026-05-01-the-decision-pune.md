For almost a year, Pin Parvati had been living in the back of my mind.

I had asked Bhaiji about shoes. I had asked him about the best time to go. I had looked at routes, photographs and stories from people who had done it.

But there was always one thing I hadn't done.

I hadn't actually decided.

Until one day in Pune, I did.

**I will do Pin Parvati this year.**

Not someday.\
Not when I'm more experienced.\
**This year.**

I told my parents.

They know me well enough to know when something is just an impulse and when I've already made up my mind.

They know I'm disciplined. They have seen me train when I didn't feel like training. They know that when it comes to travelling and trekking, I don't usually come back thinking, *"That's enough."*

I come back thinking, *"What's next?"*

And I think I know where that part of me came from.

My Dad.

He has always loved trekking. He has his own relationship with mountains, and somewhere along the way, that love found its way into me.

Maybe that's why he never really asked the question I expected him to ask:

**"What if you get tired halfway through?"**

He didn't seem worried about that.

Because perhaps he knew that being tired was never going to be the reason I stopped.

There was something strangely emotional about that for me.

My Dad had his own dreams of climbing great heights.

And now, years later, here I was, standing in front of him with a dream of my own—telling him that I wanted to spend ten days walking through the Himalayas, carrying my life on my back, crossing rivers and glaciers, and going higher than I had ever been before.

He didn't need me to prove that I wanted it.

He just asked a few questions.

My Mom, on the other hand, was thinking about the things I hadn't yet experienced.

*Will you be able to carry that much weight?*

*Will you be comfortable sleeping in tents?*

*What happens when you're exhausted?*

*Will your body actually handle ten days of this?*

Her concerns were completely different, and completely fair.

Because loving mountains from a distance is easy.

Wanting to trek is easy.

But actually waking up every morning for ten days, putting a heavy backpack on your shoulders and walking again—that is something else.

Still, after all the questions, they gave me their permission.

And suddenly, the thing that had lived in my imagination for an entire year became real.

**I was going to Pin Parvati.**

Ten days.

And I had absolutely no idea what those ten days were going to ask of me.
