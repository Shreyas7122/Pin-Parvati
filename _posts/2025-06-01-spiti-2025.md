The Spiti bike ride was dope. The roads, the altitude, the landscape—it was everything I had expected.

But somewhere along the way, I had this feeling that I could do more.

It wasn't that Spiti was easy. It just didn't feel like I had reached the edge of what I was capable of. I wanted something that would actually make me push my limits.

So I asked my tour guide,

> "Bhaiji, this isn't peak adrenaline for me. Suggest me something where I can actually feel myself pushing my limits."

That's when he started telling me about **Pin Parvati**.

At first, it was just a name.

Then he started explaining what it involved—the distance, the altitude, the terrain, the rivers, the glaciers, the isolation, and the number of days you'd spend completely away from normal life.

Something about it stayed with me.

For the next year, Pin Parvati kept coming back into my head.

I'd randomly message Bhaiji asking questions.

*Which shoes should I buy?*\
*When is the best time to go?*\
*What kind of preparation do I need?*\
*What should I expect up there?*

It slowly stopped being just an idea for an adventure.

I became fascinated by the life itself—the thought of carrying everything I needed on my back, walking for days through mountains, sleeping in tents, having almost nothing around me except the trail, the people with me and whatever the mountains decided to show us.

I wanted to live that nomadic life, even if it was only for a few days.

And somewhere during that year, Pin Parvati went from something my guide had suggested to something I knew I wanted to experience.
